# Ceiling — Expanding an Idea into Decisions

Used in **Phase 2** (creation mode). The user brought an idea and a reference (or a direction). This is the protocol that turns them into concrete design decisions — the missing mechanics between "bring an idea" and "the site has character."

Phase 2 never runs on a site that failed Phase 1, and never without the user's idea and reference. See `design-core/SKILL.md`.

## Step 1 — Decompose the reference by elements

Read the reference page as an autopsy, not as art. For each of the eight elements, answer: *what does this do here?*

- **Point** — where does the eye land first? What anchors attention?
- **Line** — what carries direction? Rules, borders, the invisible grid axis.
- **Form** — what shapes dominate? Sharp, soft, organic? One logic or mixed?
- **Color** — what mood? How many chromas? Where is the accent, and what does it point at?
- **Texture** — grain, shadow policy, material feel — or its deliberate absence?
- **Space** — where does it breathe wide, where does it tighten?
- **Typography** — what is the type doing: carrying the composition, or standing inside it?
- **Composition** — what is the gesture? Calm editorial, brutalist assertion, warm craft?

Write findings as observations, not compliments. "It's beautiful" is useless. "The type does the work the imagery usually does" is usable.

## Step 2 — Extract the gesture

Compress the decomposition into one sentence: *the world this page creates.* Not the layout — the character.

> Example: "Oversized display type on near-black, one warm elevation color, everything else silent."

This sentence is what transfers. Not the pixels, not the palette, not the font.

## Step 3 — Translate top-down through the layers

For each layer, ask: *if our site lived in this gesture, what would the decision be?*

- **Frame** — reading order, grid, gaze path, rhythm.
- **Body** — silhouette logic, radius, color tokens.
- **Surface** — typefaces, scale, texture, shadow policy.
- **Accent** — micro-states, icons, the one memorable detail.

The gesture constrains every layer. If a decision breaks the gesture, it is wrong regardless of how good it looks in isolation.

## Transfer rules

- **The principle transfers, not the pixels.** Copying the reference's palette or typeface is decoration; carrying its gesture is design. Same reference, different product, different palette, same feel — that is the test.
- **Translate, don't transplant.** The reference solves its own problem. Re-ask every decision against your material: your content, your audience, your goal from the entry steps.
- **Calibration tells stay forbidden.** The direction comes from the user; the clichés do not. Cream + serif + terracotta for "warm", purple gradient for "modern", the same display face for everything — these are tells, not gestures. The current markers are listed in `trends-2026-Q3.md`.

## Done when

Every layer decision is traceable to the gesture, and the gesture is traceable to the user's idea. If either chain breaks, stop and re-read the idea — an unfounded decision is decoration.

## What ceiling does not do

It does not invent the idea — that is the user's job and the whole reason Phase 2 requires it. It does not run without a direction — words suffice ("like X but Y"), but they must exist. It does not overrule the floor — a talented gesture never excuses broken contrast or missing focus states.
