# Design Promo — Landing Pages

A subtype lens for `design-core`. Load this after `design-core` has run its three entry steps and confirmed the goal is sale, signup, or conversion.

This file does not redefine the eight elements, the four layers, the two modes, or the two phases. It only adds what is specific to landing pages.

## Focus

The first screen is a promise: what this is, who it's for, why it matters — understood within 3 seconds. Everything below the first screen is proof and path. The gaze moves promise → proof → action.

Marketing leads by default when the goal is sale. See `design-core/references/marketing.md`. Air serves focus, not beauty.

## Typical failures

- **Multiple equal CTAs.** The eye doesn't know where to go. Fix: one primary, everything else visibly subordinate.
- **First screen about the company, not the visitor.** "We are..." instead of "You get...". Fix: rewrite from the visitor's side.
- **Proof buried at the bottom.** Trust must appear where hesitation appears, not after it.
- **Broken mobile.** Landing pages are read on phones first. A desktop-first hero fails on arrival.
- **Decorative hero.** A beautiful image that promises nothing. Fix: make the hero carry the promise, not sit behind it.

## Layer-by-layer

**Frame (composition, space).** The composition follows the gaze path — Z or F, chosen deliberately. Space groups blocks: tight space clusters related ideas, wide space separates them. The first screen owns the strongest composition on the page.

**Body (form, color).** Color carries trust or energy depending on the subject. Button forms are large and obvious — primary button visibly dominates secondary. One accent color, used almost exclusively for the primary CTA.

**Surface (texture, typography).** Typography is readable, not decorative. The headline is a real headline, not a slogan in display weight. Body text stays plain so proof (numbers, testimonials) can be scanned.

**Accent (point, line).** Icons and dividers support comprehension, not fill space. Every icon earns its place by helping the visitor understand something faster.

## Reading order

The landing page has one reading order and it does not vary by taste:

1. Promise (first screen)
2. Proof (why you can be trusted)
3. Path (the one action)

If the composition puts anything else in the top 3, revise it.
